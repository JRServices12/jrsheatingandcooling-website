It's one of the first questions people ask about air conditioning, especially in London where conservation areas and flats are common. The rules changed in 2025, and for most houses the answer is now **no**.

## The 2025 change

On 29 May 2025, the rules in England changed so that **air-to-air heat pumps**, which is what most home air conditioning systems are, get the same "permitted development" rights as other heat pumps. That means on most houses you can have a system fitted without applying for planning permission, as long as it meets the conditions. The main ones are:

- **Up to two outdoor units** on a house
- **Each outdoor unit no bigger than 1.5 cubic metres**, which covers most home systems
- The unit must meet the **noise standard** set for permitted development
- The old rule that units had to be **at least a metre from the boundary has been removed**

## When you might still need permission

- **Flats and maisonettes.** Permitted development for these works differently, and you'll usually also need permission from the freeholder or management company.
- **Listed buildings.** You'll almost always need listed building consent.
- **Conservation areas.** There are extra limits, particularly for units on a wall or roof facing the road. Parts of Greenwich, Blackheath and Chislehurst, for example, are conservation areas.
- **Outdoor units on the front of the house** facing the road can bring extra conditions.
- **Where an Article 4 direction applies,** removing some permitted development rights in that area.

If any of these apply, check with your council before work goes ahead. That will usually be Greenwich, Lewisham, Bexley, Bromley, Dartford or Gravesham.

## Where the outdoor unit usually goes

Usually on a back or side wall, on brackets or on the ground, as close to the indoor unit as possible. That keeps pipe runs short and tidy, keeps the unit away from neighbours' windows, and makes it far less likely to cause problems.
