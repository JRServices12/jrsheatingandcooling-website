When the heating comes on and a radiator isn't heating evenly, carefully feel across it (it may be hot). Where it's cold tells you most of what's going on.

## Cold at the top, warm at the bottom: air

Air rises, so a radiator that's cold across the top usually has air trapped in it. The fix is to **bleed the radiator**:

1. Turn the heating off and let the radiators cool down.
2. Put a cloth under the bleed valve at the top corner of the radiator.
3. Use a radiator key to turn the valve slowly anticlockwise. You'll hear air hiss out.
4. As soon as water starts to come out, close the valve.
5. Check your boiler pressure afterwards and top it up if it's dropped. Our guide on [boiler pressure](/blog/boiler-losing-pressure/) explains how.

If the same radiator needs bleeding every few weeks, air is getting into the system somewhere, and that's worth getting checked.

## Cold at the bottom, warm at the top: sludge

Over the years, rust and debris build up inside a heating system and form a black sludge. It's heavy, so it settles at the bottom of radiators and stops hot water getting through. Other signs include:

- Dark, dirty water when you bleed a radiator
- Radiators taking a long time to warm up
- A noisy boiler, or the boiler cutting out

Bleeding won't fix sludge. The answer is usually a **power flush**, which pumps water and cleaning chemicals through the whole system to clear it out, followed by an inhibitor to protect it. [Read more about power flushing](/services/power-flushing/).

## Cold all over

If one radiator stays completely cold while the others work, check:

- **The thermostatic valve (TRV).** Turn it fully up. TRV pins can stick after a summer off; an engineer can free or replace them.
- **The lockshield valve** at the other end. If it's been closed, the radiator won't get any flow.
- **System balancing.** Radiators nearest the boiler can take most of the flow. Balancing the system fixes this.

If no radiators are heating up, the problem is more likely the boiler, pump or a motorised valve, which needs an engineer.

## Cold in the middle

This can be a sign that sludge is building up in a radiator, or that a radiator is coming to the end of its life. It may be cheaper to replace the radiator than to keep trying to clear it.
