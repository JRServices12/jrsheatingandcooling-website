If your combi or system boiler keeps showing low pressure, or the heating cuts out with an error code, low system pressure is one of the most common causes. It's also one of the most common reasons for a call-out in South East London and North Kent once the heating goes on in autumn.

## What should my boiler pressure be?

Look at the pressure gauge on the front or underneath your boiler. With the heating **off and cold**, most boilers should sit at around **1 to 1.5 bar**. It's normal for it to rise a little when the heating is running.

- **Below 1 bar:** the pressure is low, and the boiler may lock out.
- **Above about 2.5 to 3 bar:** the pressure is too high, and the pressure relief valve may let water out.

Your boiler's manual will give the exact figure for your model.

## Common reasons boiler pressure drops

1. **You've bled the radiators.** Letting air out also lets a little pressure out. This is normal: you just need to top it back up.
2. **A small leak somewhere on the system.** Check under radiators, around valves and along visible pipework for drips, damp patches or green or white crusting on fittings.
3. **The pressure relief valve is letting by.** This valve lets water out through a small copper pipe, usually to the outside wall. If that pipe is dripping, the valve may be faulty, or the pressure is going too high when the system heats up.
4. **A faulty expansion vessel.** The vessel lets the water in your system expand as it heats. If it loses its air charge, pressure rises too high when hot and then drops when cold. This is a job for an engineer.

## Can I top up the pressure myself?

On most combi and system boilers, yes. The filling loop is usually a braided hose or a lever or key under the boiler. Your manual will show you how. Open it slowly, watch the gauge rise to around 1 to 1.5 bar, then close it fully.

> If you've topped it up and it's dropping again within days, there's an underlying fault. Keep topping it up and you'll just be adding fresh, oxygen-rich water, which speeds up corrosion inside the system. Get it looked at.

## When to call a Gas Safe engineer

- The pressure keeps dropping after you've topped it up.
- The copper pipe outside is dripping.
- You can see water coming from inside the boiler casing.
- You're not sure where the filling loop is or how to use it.

Never take the boiler's casing off yourself. Anything inside the boiler must be worked on by a Gas Safe registered engineer.
